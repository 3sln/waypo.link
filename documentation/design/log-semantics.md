# Ordered-log semantics and durable retry contract

Implemented contract in the reviewed integration candidate, with scoped local engine/HTTP/SDK and actual Worker browser evidence. This is not production or independent-provider conformance approval. Provider stores opaque append-only entries ordered by one durable per-log sequence; it does not merge application state. Apps choose their own reducers/CRDTs and independent blob references. Read/append/trim namespaces bind issuer/account/client/log/incarnation. All cursor positions and revisions use safe decimal strings/opaque tokens; see log-storage-extension.md.

## Fixed operation identity

Canonical UUIDv7 Idempotency-Key includes its immutable 48-bit Unix-ms timestamp. Initial profile: retry window 24 hours, future skew 60 seconds, maximum entry 64 KiB; all provisional. SDK mints at first network submission, persists ID+original bytes+expectedHead before sending, then never rewrites ID/timestamp after uncertain result. Offline drafts can wait without IDs. An authorized client deliberately choosing a new ID makes a new append; no semantic duplicate detection is promised.

Final transaction uses trusted wall time with a durable nondecreasing time floor. For an operation without a retained matching receipt, require issuedAt<=now+futureSkew and now<issuedAt+retryWindow. Never advance floor from caller timestamp. Clock rollback cannot revalidate a GC'd ID. Reject malformed/future key, expire typed 409 retry_window_expired before new append. Receipt GC and append use the same floor. Persist a monotonic rejectIssuedBefore timestamp. Receipt GC advances it from trusted time and the selected window before erasing any receipt; reject issuedAt below that floor even if a later policy lengthens the window. Never decrease the floor. Retain each receipt through its original issuedAt + window + skew deadline. Shrinking a window can explicitly expire uncertain attempts whose receipts are unavailable; later widening cannot reopen erased operation IDs. Restore of this floor/receipt authority is subject to coordinated rollback safety, not a new exactly-once promise.

Intent fingerprint covers exact bytes and expectedHead, including distinction between absent and present. After UUID syntax, binding and current authority checks, look up an existing receipt before applying current retry-window/reject-floor denial or current-head CAS: same ID+same intent returns the known original cursor even after other appends, trim, elapsed retry time or policy shortening, while that receipt remains available; changed intent: 409 idempotency_conflict. New operation with mismatched expectedHead: 409 head_conflict, no event/debit; caller decides a new intent, never hidden retry. Receipt retention is at least issuedAt+window+skew. No still-valid receipt eviction: finite row cap backpressures new operations while allowing existing replay. Trim/archive cannot remove dedup authority. Receipt includes retained=false if its original event was trimmed.

Expiry after ambiguous result does not prove failure. Client preserves payload/ID and reports uncertainty; only explicit application reconciliation/new append decision can obtain a new ID. Durable read entries include operationId to help app reconciliation while history remains; no permanent result lookup/dedup promise after receipt/history expiry.

## Atomic mutation and resource accounting

Append transaction rechecks namespace/current authority/lifecycle/time/intent/expectedHead/quota and commits sequence+entry+receipt+logical usage+subscription watermark together. Crash/rollback cannot publish a ghost event or allocate a successful receipt without its event. Sequence never resets or reuses, including after trim. Payload I/O before transaction requires durable owned stage/reservation and final recheck. Unknown write outcome remains charged until settled. Replay has bounded request/read work but no second logical payload debit.

TrimBefore is destructive only below explicit committed cursor. Revision CAS prevents changing history under a stale view; trim does not grant app correctness/checkpoint guarantees. Logical retained usage may fall when entries retire; physical object/SQL liabilities, receipt rows, failed deletes, export/read pins and encrypted archive duplicates remain separately bounded. No all-history synchronous cleanup. Bound archive object fetch count and bytes read independently from returned entry count and response size: a page of tiny entries cannot force unlimited segment scans or whole-object downloads. Trim never deletes independent blobs or infers unreachable app data. Zero-byte entries consume row/receipt/work stock.

Archive immutable bounded ranges; verify object bytes/hash, then CAS incarnation/range/floor/manifest revision. Concurrent append beyond range survives. A delayed archive cannot restore trimmed entries; losing object remains durably owned garbage. Capture page highWater across all pages so continuous writers do not prevent catch-up. Reads/export pins, trim floor and GC must share a complete ownership protocol: after awaited object reads recheck live authority/floor; never turn missing bytes into empty success. Archive maintenance has bounded prepaid work and lifecycle fencing, independent of browser cookies. Crashes at each publication/delete boundary must retain data or owned cleanup liability.

## Qualification and remaining deployment gates

Pure ngin: duplicate/conflicting ID, expectedHead replay ordering, clock rollback and policy changes, inclusive through/exclusive after, empty origin snapshot, trim boundary/CAS, retry aftertrim, receipt backpressure, overflow and namespace substitution. Object/adapters: delayed archive publication versus trim, held read versus deletion, lost/partial PUT and rollback/restart, quota/physical liabilities, accepted authority revocation during I/O. HTTP/SDK: exact DTO/status/body limits, new consent scopes, signed actor dispatch, account switch/outbox uncertainty and reconnect ranges. Full engine/runtime/browser gates remain required; tiny proof alone is not archival or production evidence.


## Tail resume uses the same retained-range contract

The public [CBOR-sequence tail contract](log-storage-extension.md#public-tail-request-and-response) defines exact ready/entry/terminal maps and physical reconnect rules. A tail is an ordered replay after an explicit cursor followed by live commits, not an invalidation hint or latest-value feed. `ready.highWater` is not permission to skip catch-up. State and the processed cursor commit atomically before local ACK; no provider checkpoint/ACK endpoint is implied. Reconnect carries the last processed cursor. Transport credit and provider delivery state never advance it.

Retained receipt lookup still precedes retry-window/reject-floor/head-CAS denial for the same already-known append intent; tail reception does not change that append contract. Current authority and namespace validation precede any receipt disclosure. Do not turn temporary stock pressure or EOF into a new append ID, a new trim revision or omission of a rejected resume cursor.

Finite reads and tail opening share these important edge cases:

- Explicit `after` below floor fails, even when it is the immediate predecessor, `after>=through`, or `through` is origin. Validate binding/future/retention before returning an empty range.
- Omitted `after` deliberately starts at the current retained floor; it does not promise history since log creation and is never an automatic stale-resume fallback.
- `through=origin` with no explicit stale `after` requests a stable empty snapshot even when later appends exist. It cannot bypass stale-after checks.
- A valid retained `after>=through` yields an empty bounded page after validation. `through` remains the inclusive fixed highWater across pagination; never replace it with a newer head to make a failed snapshot look successful.

Physical archival is transparent and does not reclaim idempotency receipts or create an account recovery archive. Paid dormancy/download/restore remains separately unimplemented under the [paid archive contract](paid-dormancy-archive-contract.md). Local qualification cannot establish deployed hibernation or billing cost.
